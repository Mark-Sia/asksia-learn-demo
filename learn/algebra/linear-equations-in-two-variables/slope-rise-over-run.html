Slope: Rise over Run

How steep is a line? Count up, count across, divide — that is the whole idea.

A 5-minute interactive walk from two points to the slope formula: build the rise-run triangle on the graph, flip the sign, break the formula on a vertical line, and drag the points yourself until m makes sense.

Slope measures how steep a line is. It is the rise (change in y) divided by the run (change in x) between any two points on the line: m = (y2 − y1)/(x2 − x1). A line through (−2, −1) and (3, 2) rises 3 while it runs 5, so its slope is 3/5 = 0.6 — and the sign of that number tells you whether the line climbs or falls.

Key takeaways

  • The slope formula: Slope is rise over run: m = (y2 − y1)/(x2 − x1). For (−2, −1) and (3, 2): rise 3, run 5, so m = 3/5 = 0.6. Exam tip: Put y on top. Writing run/rise instead of rise/run is the #1 slope error on SPR (student-produced response) questions.
  • Sign = direction: Read the sign from the picture: climbing left to right ⇒ m > 0; falling ⇒ m < 0; flat ⇒ m = 0. Check your computed sign against the graph every time.
  • Same point first: In both differences the same point goes first. Mixing the order flips the sign: 3/−5 = −0.6 instead of 3/5 = 0.6. Flip both or flip neither.
  • Horizontal vs vertical: Horizontal line: rise 0 ⇒ m = 0. Vertical line: run 0 ⇒ division by zero ⇒ slope is undefined — not 0, not infinity.
  • The m in y = mx + b: In y = mx + b, the coefficient of x IS the slope: go 1 right, go up by m. Our line is y = 0.6x + 0.2. Exam tip: The SAT loves unit-rate-as-slope: cost per ride, km per minute, pages per hour. In any linear model, that per-one-unit rate IS m — find it with rise over run.

Lesson transcript

  1. Here is a line, and two points sitting on it: P1 at (−2, −1) and P2 at (3, 2). Every SAT asks some version of one question about this picture: how steep is the line? Not roughly — exactly, as a single number. That number is the slope, and by the end of this lesson you will read it straight off any two points.
  2. To measure steepness, walk from P1 to P2 in two moves. First slide right: from x = −2 to x = 3 is 5 steps — that is the run. Then climb up: from y = −1 to y = 2 is 3 steps — that is the rise. Together the two moves make a right triangle hiding under the line, and that triangle IS the steepness.
  3. Now the formula. Slope m equals rise over run: m = (y2 − y1) / (x2 − x1). Plug our points in: (2 − (−1)) over (3 − (−2)) — that is 3 over 5, so m = 0.6. Read it as a rate: every time you go 1 to the right, the line climbs 0.6. That single fraction is the whole formula.
  4. The sign of m is the direction of the line. Watch P2 move. Up at (3, 2), the line climbs left to right — m is 0.6, positive, green. Drop P2 level with P1 and the line goes flat — the rise is 0, so m is 0. Drop it further, to (3, −4), and now the line falls — m is −0.6, negative, red. Up is plus, flat is zero, down is minus. You can read the sign before you compute anything.
  5. Your turn. Grab P2 and drag it anywhere on the grid. The triangle redraws, rise and run recount themselves, and m recomputes live. Push the line steep, flatten it, flip it downhill — watch the number and the color answer you.
  6. Now the trap that costs real points. The formula only works if the SAME point goes first in both differences. The card always plays it safe — same point first: P2 on top and P2 on the bottom gives (2 − (−1)) over (3 − (−2)) — 3 over 5, m = 0.6. But on your scratch paper, mix them — y from the second point, x from the first — and you get 3 over −5: m = −0.6. Wrong sign, same line, wrong answer. Flip BOTH or flip neither: (−1 − 2) over (−2 − 3) is −3 over −5, which is 0.6 again.
  7. Two special lines, straight from the formula. Slide P2 level with P1, to (3, −1): the rise is 0, so m is 0 over 5 — zero. Every horizontal line has slope exactly 0. Now slide P2 directly above P1, to (−2, 2): the run is 0, and m would be 3 over 0. You cannot divide by zero — a vertical line has an undefined slope. Not zero, not infinity — your answer is the word undefined. Finally, let P2 settle back where it belongs.
  8. Time to build, not just watch. I will name a slope — you drag the points until the readout matches, then let go. Three targets, each a little trickier.
  9. One last connection, and it makes equations easy. Every non-vertical line can be written y = mx + b — slope-intercept form, the SAT's favorite outfit for a line — and that m is exactly the slope you have been dragging. Our line, through (−2, −1) and (3, 2), has m = 0.6 and crosses x = 0 at height 0.2, so it is y = 0.6x + 0.2. When the SAT hands you an equation, the slope is just sitting there in front of the x — and now you know what that number is doing.
  10. Look at the full picture one more time. Two points, a right triangle, one fraction: rise over run, same point first in both differences. The sign is the direction, flat means zero, vertical means undefined, and the m in y = mx + b is this exact number. Frequently asked questions

What is the slope formula?

m = (y2 − y1)/(x2 − x1) — the change in y divided by the change in x between any two points on the line. It is the same thing as rise over run.

What does rise over run mean?

Rise is how far the line goes up or down between two points; run is how far it goes across. Their ratio, rise/run, is the slope. From (−2, −1) to (3, 2) the rise is 3 and the run is 5, so m = 3/5 = 0.6.

Why is my slope negative?

Either the line really falls from left to right (down = negative, that is correct), or you mixed the order of subtraction — the same point must go first in both differences, or the sign flips.

What is the slope of a horizontal line?

Zero. Between any two points on a horizontal line the rise is 0, so m = 0 divided by the run = 0.

What is the slope of a vertical line?

Undefined. The run between any two points on a vertical line is 0, and division by zero is undefined — it is not 0 and not infinity.

Does it matter which point I call (x1, y1)?

No — either point can be first, as long as you keep the SAME order on top and bottom. (2 − (−1))/(3 − (−2)) and (−1 − 2)/(−2 − 3) both give 0.6.
