Sine & Cosine

The circle behind the wave

Sine and cosine are not just calculator buttons or the triangle rule SOH-CAH-TOA — they are the coordinates of a single point moving around the unit circle (a circle of radius 1). For a point at angle θ, cos θ is its x-coordinate and sin θ is its y-coordinate. Sweeping θ traces the sine and cosine waves.

Key takeaways

  • The definition: On the unit circle, cos θ is the x-coordinate of the point and sin θ is its y-coordinate. Exam tip: Because the point stays on a radius-1 circle, sin θ and cos θ are always between −1 and 1.
  • The triangle is inside: cos θ and sin θ are the horizontal and vertical legs of a right triangle with hypotenuse 1 — SOH-CAH-TOA and the unit circle are the same picture.
  • The special angles: 0°→(1,0), 30°→(√3⁄2, 1⁄2), 45°→(√2⁄2, √2⁄2), 60°→(1⁄2, √3⁄2), 90°→(0,1). Read (cos, sin) straight off the point. Exam tip: Cofunction identity: sin θ = cos(90° − θ) — they swap across complementary angles.
  • The wave: Sweeping θ traces sin θ as a smooth wave; cos θ is the same wave shifted by 90° — cos θ = sin(θ + 90°).

Frequently asked questions

What is cosine on the unit circle?

cos θ is the x-coordinate of the point at angle θ on the unit circle.

What is sine on the unit circle?

sin θ is the y-coordinate of the point at angle θ on the unit circle.

Why are sine and cosine always between -1 and 1?

Because the point never leaves the radius-1 unit circle, so neither coordinate can exceed 1 or drop below -1.

What are sin 90° and cos 90°?

sin 90° = 1 and cos 90° = 0 — the point sits at the top of the circle, (0, 1).

How are the sine and cosine waves related?

They are the same shape shifted by 90 degrees: cos θ = sin(θ + 90°).

What is sin 45°?

sin 45° = √2⁄2 ≈ 0.707, and it equals cos 45°.
